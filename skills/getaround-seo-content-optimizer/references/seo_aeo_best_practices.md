# SEO & AEO Best Practices for Getaround Content

Comprehensive guidelines for optimizing content for both traditional search engines AND AI overviews/LLM training data.

## Part 1: Traditional SEO Fundamentals

### Keyword Research & Targeting

**Primary keyword selection:**
- One main keyword per article
- Must have search volume (check GSC for existing impressions)
- Must match Getaround's business (avoid vanity traffic)
- Include in: H1, intro, one H2/H3, conclusion, meta description

**Secondary keywords:**
- 2-4 related keywords
- Natural variations and long-tail
- Weave naturally throughout body

**Keyword placement priorities:**
1. H1 (title) - MUST include main keyword
2. First 100 words - Include main keyword early
3. One H2 or H3 - Reinforce main keyword
4. Conclusion - Include main keyword
5. Meta description - Include main keyword
6. Throughout body - Natural frequency (avoid stuffing)

**Keyword density:**
- Main keyword: 0.5-2% density (natural)
- Avoid exact-match repetition - use variations
- Focus on natural language, not keyword stuffing

### Search Intent Matching

**4 types of search intent:**

1. **Informational** - "how to", "what is", "guide to"
   - Format: Educational, comprehensive guides
   - Goal: Answer the question thoroughly
   - CTA: Soft (explore more, see related)

2. **Navigational** - "getaround [city]", "getaround login"
   - Format: Direct, concise, clear next steps
   - Goal: Get user to destination quickly
   - CTA: Direct (find cars, book now)

3. **Transactional** - "rent car [city]", "lease [car type]"
   - Format: Product-focused, comparison, pricing
   - Goal: Drive conversion
   - CTA: Strong (book now, see prices, find car)

4. **Commercial investigation** - "best car rental", "[car type] vs [car type]"
   - Format: Comparison, reviews, pros/cons
   - Goal: Help decision-making
   - CTA: Medium (explore options, compare prices)

**Always match content format to search intent.**

### Title Tag (H1) Optimization

**Best practices:**
- 50-60 characters (display fully in SERP)
- Include main keyword at start
- Make it compelling (not just keyword)
- Use power words: "ultimate", "complete", "comprehensive", "best", "topp"
- Include number if listicle: "Topp 5", "10 best"
- Include location if relevant: "i Oslo", "i Norge"

**Examples:**
- ✅ "Topp 5 aktiviteter for barn i Oslo | 2025 guide"
- ✅ "Bli profesjonell bilutleier: komplett guide"
- ✅ "Langtidsleie av bil i Norge: alt du må vite"
- ❌ "Aktiviteter" (too vague)
- ❌ "Guide til å leie ut bil på Getaround med tips og triks for å optimalisere inntekt" (too long)

### Meta Description Optimization

**Best practices:**
- 150-160 characters
- Include main keyword
- Include compelling benefit/hook
- Include call-to-action
- Match search intent

**Examples:**
- ✅ "Oppdag de beste aktivitetene for barn i Oslo. Komplett guide med parking, priser og tips. Planlegg den perfekte familiedagen!" (157 chars)
- ✅ "Lei ut bilen din på Getaround og tjen 8000-15000 kr/mnd. Komplett guide til prising, forsikring og optimalisering." (156 chars)

### Header Structure (H2/H3/H4)

**Best practices:**
- H1: One per article (the title)
- H2: Main sections (4-8 per article)
- H3: Subsections under H2 (as needed)
- H4: Rarely needed (only for deep nesting)

**Header hierarchy rules:**
- Never skip levels (H2 → H4 is wrong)
- Use descriptive headers (not just "Section 1")
- Include keywords naturally in 1-2 headers
- Make headers scannable (user should understand article from headers alone)

**Example structure:**
```
# H1: Topp 5 aktiviteter for barn i Oslo

## H2: Hvorfor Oslo er perfekt for familier
## H2: 1. Vikingskipshuset
### H3: Praktisk info
### H3: Tips for besøket
## H2: 2. Tusenfryd
### H3: Praktisk info
### H3: Tips for besøket
## H2: Planlegg turen med leiebil
## H2: Ofte stilte spørsmål
```

### Internal Linking Strategy

**Why internal linking matters:**
- Helps Google understand site structure
- Distributes page authority
- Keeps users on site longer
- Improves crawlability

**Best practices:**
- 3-5 internal links per article (minimum)
- Link to related articles (same topic cluster)
- Link from high-traffic articles to "Potential" articles (boost their ranking)
- Use descriptive anchor text (not "click here")
- Link contextually (within body text, not just sidebar)

**Example:**
- ❌ "For mer info, klikk her"
- ✅ "Les vår guide til langtidsleie av bil for lengre opphold"

**Strategic linking priorities:**
1. From "Stars" (top 10 articles) → "Potential" articles
2. From activity guides → related activity guides in other cities
3. From renter content → owner content (and vice versa) where relevant
4. From general → specific (e.g., "bildeling" → "bildeling i Oslo")

### Content Length & Depth

**Length by content type:**
- **Listicles (activities)**: 1,200-1,800 words (150-200 words per item)
- **How-to guides (owner)**: 1,800-2,500 words (comprehensive)
- **Use-case articles**: 800-1,200 words (focused)
- **City guides**: 2,000-3,000 words (comprehensive, 20+ activities)

**Depth requirements:**
- Answer the question completely (don't leave user needing more info)
- Include practical details (parking, prices, season, tips)
- Add unique insights (data, expert quotes, local knowledge)
- Balance thoroughness with readability (use headers, short paragraphs)

### Readability & Formatting

**Paragraph length:**
- 2-4 sentences per paragraph (mobile-friendly)
- Mix short and medium paragraphs
- Break up long sections with headers

**Sentence length:**
- 15-20 words average
- Mix short (5-10) and medium (15-25) sentences
- Avoid long, complex sentences

**Formatting elements:**
- **Bold** key terms and takeaways (sparingly)
- Use bullet lists for 3+ items
- Use numbered lists for steps or rankings
- Use blockquotes for important callouts
- Add tables for comparisons or data

**Scannable content:**
- User should understand article from headers + bold text alone
- First sentence of each section = key takeaway
- Last sentence of intro = promise ("In this article, you'll learn...")

### Image Optimization

**Best practices:**
- Include 1 hero image (top of article)
- 1-2 images per major section
- Alt text with keywords (naturally)
- Descriptive file names (not "IMG_1234.jpg")
- Compressed for fast loading

**Alt text examples:**
- ✅ "Barn som leker i Oslo Sommerpark"
- ✅ "Tesla Model 3 parkert ved Operaen i Oslo"
- ❌ "Image 1"

## Part 2: AEO (Answer Engine Optimization)

### Understanding AEO

**What is AEO?**
AEO = Answer Engine Optimization = Optimizing content for AI overviews, LLM training data, and voice search.

**Why it matters:**
- Google SGE (Search Generative Experience) shows AI-generated overviews
- ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity cite sources in responses
- Voice search uses AI to parse and answer
- Being cited in AI responses = massive visibility boost

**Key difference from SEO:**
- SEO: Optimize to rank in traditional search results (list of links)
- AEO: Optimize to be cited/featured in AI-generated answers

### E-E-A-T Signals (Critical for AEO)

**E-E-A-T = Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness**

Google (and LLMs) use E-E-A-T to determine which sources to cite.

**How to demonstrate E-E-A-T:**

1. **Experience** (new "E")
   - Share first-hand experience: "I've rented out my Tesla in Oslo for 2 years"
   - Include personal insights: "In my experience, weekends book fastest"
   - Show real usage: "Our users report that..."

2. **Expertise**
   - Show domain knowledge: Specific data, statistics, insider info
   - Use proper terminology correctly
   - Explain complex topics clearly
   - Reference authoritative sources

3. **Authoritativeness**
   - Author bio (if applicable)
   - Company credentials (Getaround = largest platform in Norway)
   - Data from proprietary sources (Getaround booking data)
   - Partnerships (insurance partner, etc.)

4. **Trustworthiness**
   - Transparency (acknowledge limitations, price variations)
   - Accurate information (fact-check, verify data)
   - Clear disclaimers (where appropriate)
   - Contact information available

### FAQ Schema & Structured Data

**Why FAQ schema matters:**
- Featured snippets in Google
- "People Also Ask" inclusion
- AI overview citation
- Voice search answers

**Best practices:**
- Include 3-7 FAQ questions at article end
- Answer briefly (50-100 words per answer)
- Use natural question phrasing: "Hvor mye koster det å leie bil i Oslo?"
- Include long-tail keywords in questions
- Implement JSON-LD schema (technical: developer implements, but content must be FAQ-formatted)

**Example FAQ section:**
```markdown
## Ofte stilte spørsmål

### Hvor mye koster det å leie bil i Oslo?
Prisen varierer fra 200-800 kr per dag avhengig av biltype. En liten elbil koster typisk 200-350 kr/dag, mens en Tesla eller stor SUV kan koste 600-800 kr/dag. Langtidsleie gir ofte 20-30% rabatt.

### Trenger jeg forsikring når jeg leier bil på Getaround?
Ja, all forsikring er inkludert i leieprisen. Getaround tilbyr tre forsikringsnivåer som reduserer egenandelen fra 8000 kr (standard) til 3000 kr eller 0 kr mot en daglig ekstrakostnad.

### Hvordan fungerer Getaround Connect?
Med Getaround Connect åpner og starter du bilen direkte med appen - ingen fysisk nøkkelutveksling. Bilen har en installert boks som gjør at du kan låse opp og starte bilen med telefonen.
```

### Unique Data Points & Statistics

**Why unique data matters:**
- LLMs prioritize sources with original data
- Increases citation likelihood
- Builds authority
- Creates "linkable" content

**What counts as unique data:**
- Proprietary booking data ("80% of Oslo rentals are EVs")
- User survey results ("Our users report...")
- Pricing analysis from internal data
- Trend analysis ("Summer bookings increased 40% year-over-year")
- Local market insights

**How to present data:**
- Lead with the number: "80% of bookings in Oslo are electric cars"
- Include source: "Based on Getaround Norway booking data from 2024"
- Add context: "This makes Oslo one of Europe's highest EV adoption markets for car-sharing"
- Visualize when possible (tables, lists)

**Example:**
```markdown
## Elbil-andelen i Oslo billeie

**80% av alle billeie i Oslo er nå elbiler**, basert på Getarounds bookingdata fra 2024. Dette gjør Oslo til en av Europas ledende byer for elektrisk bildeling.

De mest populære elbilene:
1. Tesla Model 3 - 35% av bookinger
2. VW ID.4 - 15% av bookinger  
3. Nissan Leaf - 12% av bookinger
```

### Expert Quotes & Attributions

**Why expert quotes matter:**
- Increases E-E-A-T (authority)
- LLMs prefer attributed information
- Adds credibility
- Provides diverse perspectives

**Who counts as "expert":**
- Getaround team members (product, ops, CS)
- Power users (owners with >100 trips)
- Local tourism experts
- Industry analysts

**How to include:**
- Name + title: "Ifølge Per Olsen, produktsjef hos Getaround Norge..."
- Quote + attribution: "Langhelger booker alltid ut først," sier Maria Hansen, som har leid ut sin Tesla i Oslo i 3 år."
- Expert insight: "Tourism bureau i Oslo bekrefter at..."

**Example:**
```markdown
"Familier søker ofte etter aktiviteter som kombinerer læring og lek," forklarer Anna Larsen, som har jobbet med familieturisme i Oslo i 15 år. "Vikingskipshuset og Naturhistorisk museum treffer perfekt denne balansen."
```

### Content Freshness Signals

**Why freshness matters:**
- Google prioritizes recent content for time-sensitive queries
- LLMs check publication/update dates
- Users trust updated information

**How to signal freshness:**
- Include year in title: "Topp 5 aktiviteter i Oslo 2025"
- Update date in metadata
- Reference recent events/data: "I 2024 økte..."
- Review/update content every 6-12 months
- Add "Sist oppdatert: [dato]" at top

**Content types requiring regular updates:**
- Pricing information
- Seasonal guides
- "Best of" lists
- Statistical data
- Regulatory/rule changes

### Semantic Richness & Related Entities

**What is semantic richness?**
Using related terms, concepts, and entities that help AI understand topic fully.

**Example: Article about "leie bil Oslo"**

**Semantically poor:**
"Du kan leie bil i Oslo. Billeie i Oslo er populært. Oslo har mange biler til leie."

**Semantically rich:**
"Du kan leie bil i Oslo fra flere aktører som Getaround, Hyre og tradisjonelle utleieselskaper. Bildeling via peer-to-peer-plattformer har vokst kraftig, spesielt i bydeler som Grünerløkka, Majorstuen og Frogner. Elbiler utgjør 80% av tilbudet, med Tesla Model 3 som mest populære modell."

**How to increase semantic richness:**
- Use related terms (synonyms, variations)
- Mention related entities (locations, brands, models)
- Include co-occurring concepts (insurance, booking, parking)
- Add contextual information (why, when, who, how)

## Part 3: Getaround-Specific Integration

### USP Integration (Natural, Not Pushy)

**7 key USPs to weave into content:**

1. **Proximity** - 8000+ cars across Norway
   - Weave: "Med biler tilgjengelig i alle bydeler i Oslo..."
   - Avoid: "Getaround har 8000 biler! Book nå!"

2. **Convenience** - Book in minutes
   - Weave: "Du kan booke bil på under 2 minutter via appen"
   - Avoid: "Så enkelt! Så raskt! Book nå!"

3. **Flexibility** - Hourly to 90 days
   - Weave: "Enten du trenger bil i 3 timer eller 3 uker, prisen justeres automatisk"
   - Avoid: "Fleksibel leie! Fra time til måned!"

4. **Connect** - Unlock with phone
   - Weave: "Med Connect-biler slipper du fysisk nøkkelutveksling - alt skjer via telefonen"
   - Avoid: "Connect er så smart! Prøv nå!"

5. **Price** - Up to 40% cheaper
   - Weave: "Bildeling er ofte 30-40% rimeligere enn tradisjonelle utleieselskaper"
   - Avoid: "BILLIGST! 40% rabatt!"

6. **Insurance** - Full coverage included
   - Weave: "Forsikring er inkludert, med mulighet for redusert egenandel"
   - Avoid: "Tryggest i Norge!"

7. **Environment** - Car-sharing reduces car ownership
   - Weave: "Bildeling reduserer behovet for privatbilisme og gir mer bærekraftig mobilitet"
   - Avoid: "Redd planeten! Del bil!"

**Golden rule:** Integrate USPs as facts/benefits, not marketing copy. Show, don't sell.

### CTA (Call-to-Action) Guidelines

**Strength by content type:**

**Informational content (activities, guides):**
- Soft CTA: "Klar for å utforske Oslo? Finn perfekt bil på Getaround."
- Placement: End of article only

**Transactional content (rent car, specific use):**
- Strong CTA: "Finn bil til bryllupet ditt: Se tilgjengelige biler i Oslo"
- Placement: Middle + end

**Owner content:**
- Medium CTA: "Klar til å begynne? Registrer din bil på Getaround og start å tjene."
- Placement: After benefits section + end

**Best practices:**
- One CTA per article (two max for long articles)
- Make it contextual (not generic "book now")
- Link to relevant page (Oslo cars, owner signup, specific car type)
- No ALL CAPS or excessive punctuation

### Tone Calibration

**Renter content:**
- Friendly, warm, inspiring
- "Du", "din", "deg" (informal)
- Evoke feelings: freedom, adventure, convenience
- Paint pictures: sensory language
- Be helpful: practical tips, insider knowledge

**Example:**
"Forestill deg å kjøre langs Sognefjorden med vinduene ned, fjellene reiser seg på begge sider, og du stopper akkurat når du vil for å ta bilder eller spise lunsj ved vannet. Med leiebil får du friheten til å utforske i ditt eget tempo."

**Owner content:**
- Professional, data-driven, concrete
- Still "du", but more business-like
- Focus on ROI, optimization, scalability
- Use numbers: "8000 kr/mnd", "80% utnyttelse"
- Be direct: clear steps, actionable advice

**Example:**
"En Tesla Model 3 i Oslo kan generere 8.000-15.000 kr per måned i passive inntekter, avhengig av tilgjengelighet og prising. Med riktig optimalisering av pris og tilgjengelighet ser vi at de beste utleierne oppnår 70-85% utnyttelse."

## Part 4: Markdown Formatting for Ghost

### Required Format

All articles must be output as **100% valid Markdown** ready to paste into Ghost CMS.

### Basic Markdown Elements

**Headers:**
```markdown
# H1 - Article title (one per article)
## H2 - Main sections
### H3 - Subsections
#### H4 - Rarely needed
```

**Paragraphs:**
- Separate paragraphs with blank line
- No indentation

**Emphasis:**
```markdown
**Bold text**
*Italic text* (use sparingly)
```

**Lists:**
```markdown
Unordered list:
- Item 1
- Item 2
- Item 3

Ordered list:
1. First item
2. Second item
3. Third item
```

**Links:**
```markdown
[Link text](https://url.com)
```

**Images:**
```markdown
![Alt text description](image-url-or-placeholder)
```

**Blockquotes:**
```markdown
> Important callout or quote
```

**Tables:**
```markdown
| Header 1 | Header 2 | Header 3 |
|----------|----------|----------|
| Cell 1   | Cell 2   | Cell 3   |
| Cell 4   | Cell 5   | Cell 6   |
```

## Part 6: Common Mistakes to Avoid

### SEO Mistakes
❌ Keyword stuffing ("leiebil oslo leiebil oslo leiebil oslo")
❌ Irrelevant keywords for vanity traffic
❌ Too-long title tags (truncated in SERP)
❌ Generic meta descriptions ("Read more about...")
❌ No internal links
❌ Skipping header levels (H2 → H4)
❌ Images without alt text

### AEO Mistakes
❌ No FAQ section
❌ Vague, general content (no unique data)
❌ No attribution/sources
❌ Outdated information without freshness signals
❌ Poor E-E-A-T demonstration

### Content Mistakes
❌ Content-product mismatch (writing about topics unrelated to car rental)
❌ Too commercial/pushy (excessive CTAs, marketing language)
❌ Wrong tone for audience (casual for owners, corporate for renters)
❌ Too long paragraphs (wall of text)
❌ Missing practical information (prices, parking, tips)
❌ No clear promise/value proposition
❌ Weak hook (boring intro)

### Getaround-Specific Mistakes
❌ Not validating business fit (vanity traffic)
❌ Ignoring geographic priorities (writing about small cities)
❌ Using wrong format (case study when listicle would work better)
❌ Not integrating USPs naturally
❌ Missing conversion opportunities
❌ Not leveraging proven patterns from data insights

## Part 9: AI "Interview" Method for Unique Content

### The Problem: Generic AI Content

AI-generated content often lacks uniqueness because it draws from the same publicly available information. The result is content that sounds similar to competitors and offers no distinctive value.

### The Solution: AI Interview Technique

Use AI as an "interviewer" to systematically extract unique expertise, experiences, and insights from subject matter experts at Getaround. This creates content that competitors cannot replicate.

### How It Works

**Step 1: Identify the Expert**
- Product managers for feature-related content
- Owner relations team for owner content
- Customer support for FAQ and common questions
- Marketing team for brand/positioning content
- Fleet managers for vehicle-specific insights

**Step 2: Run the AI Interview**

Use this prompt template to extract unique insights:

```
You are conducting an expert interview to gather unique insights for a blog article about [TOPIC].

The interviewee is [NAME/ROLE], a [JOB TITLE] at Getaround with [X years] of experience in [RELEVANT AREA].

Ask 10 thoughtful questions that will elicit:
1. Unique data points not available publicly
2. Behind-the-scenes insights about how things work
3. Real customer stories or examples (anonymized)
4. Expert predictions or opinions
5. Practical tips from experience
6. Common mistakes and how to avoid them
7. Industry insider knowledge
8. Comparative insights (what makes Getaround different)
9. Future developments or trends
10. Counterintuitive findings or surprises

For each answer, follow up with "Can you give me a specific example?" or "Do you have any data on that?"

Format the output as Q&A pairs that can be directly quoted in the article.
```

**Step 3: Extract Quotable Content**

From the interview, identify:
- Specific numbers and data points
- Named examples (with permission)
- Expert quotes to attribute
- Counterintuitive insights
- "Insider tips" for listicles

### Getaround-Specific Interview Topics

**For Renter Content:**
- Most popular car types by use case (wedding, moving, road trip)
- Peak booking times and availability patterns
- Hidden costs to avoid (from customer support data)
- Best practices from high-rated renters

**For Owner Content:**
- Income optimization strategies from top earners
- Most common owner mistakes (from support data)
- Pricing strategies that work
- Fleet management tips from professional owners

**For City Guides:**
- Local parking tips from frequent users in that city
- Best routes for popular road trips
- Seasonal patterns and availability

### Quality Markers for Interview-Based Content

Content based on AI interviews should include:

- [ ] At least 3 specific data points not available elsewhere
- [ ] At least 2 direct quotes with attribution
- [ ] At least 1 "insider tip" or counterintuitive insight
- [ ] Real examples (anonymized if needed)
- [ ] Expert perspective clearly identified

### Example Interview Output

**Topic:** "How to maximize rental income on Getaround"

**Interview Q:** What's the single biggest mistake new owners make?

**Expert A (Fleet Manager):** "The biggest mistake is setting prices too low initially. New owners think lower prices will get more bookings, but in our data, owners who price within 10% of market rate actually get 40% more bookings than those who undercut by 30% or more. Low prices signal to renters that something might be wrong with the car."

**Used in article:**
> "According to Getaround's Fleet Manager, owners who price within 10% of market rate get 40% more bookings than those who significantly undercut. 'Low prices signal to renters that something might be wrong with the car,' they note."

## Part 10: Internal Linking Strategy

### Why Internal Linking Matters

Internal links:
- Distribute page authority throughout the site
- Help search engines discover and index pages
- Improve user navigation and session duration
- Create topical clusters that boost authority

### Internal Linking Prompt Template

Use this prompt to identify internal linking opportunities when optimizing content:

```
I need to identify internal linking opportunities for the Getaround blog.

**Article being optimized:**
URL: [CURRENT ARTICLE URL]
Topic: [MAIN TOPIC]
Primary keyword: [PRIMARY KEYWORD]

**Existing blog articles to potentially link to:**
1. [URL 1] - [Title 1]
2. [URL 2] - [Title 2]
3. [URL 3] - [Title 3]
[... list all relevant existing articles]

**Task:**
1. Identify 3-5 natural places in the current article where we can link to other blog posts
2. For each opportunity, specify:
   - The anchor text to use (should be natural, not keyword-stuffed)
   - The exact sentence where the link should be placed
   - The target URL to link to
   - Why this link makes sense (contextual relevance)

3. Also identify which existing articles should link TO this article (reverse internal links)

**Guidelines:**
- Links should feel natural to the reader
- Anchor text should be descriptive but not over-optimized
- Prioritize links that help the user's journey
- Avoid linking to unrelated content
- Target 3-5 outbound internal links per 1000 words
```

### Internal Linking Best Practices

**Link Placement:**
- Early in the article (within first 300 words) for important links
- In the body where contextually relevant
- In the "Related reading" section at the end
- NOT in headers or CTAs

**Anchor Text Guidelines:**
- ✅ Natural phrases: "learn more about renting a car for your wedding"
- ✅ Descriptive: "our guide to Oslo activities"
- ❌ Exact match keyword spam: "rent car Oslo best price cheap"
- ❌ Generic: "click here", "read more"

**Link Quantity:**
- 3-5 internal links per 1000 words
- No more than 2 links in any single paragraph
- Ensure links go to genuinely relevant content

### Getaround Blog Link Clusters

**Renter Topic Clusters:**
- City activity guides (Oslo, Bergen, Tromsø)
- Use-case guides (wedding, moving, road trips)
- Comparison content (Getaround vs. alternatives)
- How-to guides (booking, insurance, Connect)

**Owner Topic Clusters:**
- Getting started guides
- Income optimization
- Vehicle care and maintenance
- Tax and insurance guides

**Cross-Cluster Linking:**
- Renter content can link to owner content where relevant
- Example: Activity guide → "Learn how owners keep these cars in top shape"

### Internal Link Audit Checklist

When optimizing an article:

- [ ] Article has 3-5 internal links per 1000 words
- [ ] Links use natural, descriptive anchor text
- [ ] Links point to relevant, high-quality pages
- [ ] No broken internal links
- [ ] Key pages (booking, owner signup) are accessible within 2 clicks
- [ ] Article is linked FROM at least 2-3 other relevant articles
- [ ] No orphan pages (pages with zero internal links pointing to them)
